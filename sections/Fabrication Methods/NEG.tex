\section{Alkali Vapor Dispensing and Non-Evaporable Gas Gettering}
\label{sec:neg_dispenser}
\textbf{CHECK CITATIONS, CHECK CEHMICAL  REACTIONS}
Prior to the final hermetic sealing of the laser-written vapor cell, a
small solid-state non-evaporable getter (NEG) dispenser pill 
is precisely positioned within the
micromachined reservoir cavity. The cell enclosure is
subsequently sealed inside a controlled chamber filled with atmospheric
pressure nitrogen (\ce{N2}) buffer gas, ensuring an ultra-low oxygen (\ce{O2})
concentration below \SI{6}{ppm} to avoid trapping reactive oxygen species within
the internal cavities \cite{lucivero2022}. Throughout this initial assembly
stage, the solid-state pill remains completely stable and chemically inert,
preventing any premature release of reactive species before the hermetic
enclosure is fully secured.

The dispenser pill consists of a compressed powder mixture combining an
alkali-metal chromate, such as rubidium chromate (\ce{RbCrO4}) or cesium
chromate (\ce{CsCrO4}), with a specialized reducing agent \cite{scherer2012}.
The reducing agent, commercially designated as SAES St101, is composed of
\SI{84}{\percent} zirconium (Zr) and \SI{16}{\percent} aluminum (Al) by weight
\cite{scherer2012}. While these dispenser materials are traditionally activated
in macroscopic vacuum systems via ohmic heating using currents in the range of
\SI{3}{\ampere} to \SI{7}{\ampere} to drive the pellet into a temperature range
of \SI{400}{\celsius} to \SI{800}{\celsius} \cite{scherer2012}, the sub-millimeter
LWVC architecture utilizes an on-demand, non-invasive laser activation
mechanism to trigger the reduction-oxidation process post-sealing \cite{lucivero2022}.
Under this thermal activation, the chemical reduction
of the rubidium chromate by the zirconium matrix follows the stoichiometry \cite{knapkiewicz2018}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{4Rb_2CrO_4 + 5Zr \rightarrow 8Rb\uparrow + 2Cr_2O_3 + 5ZrO_2}
    \label{eq:reduction_rb}
\end{equation}

To initiate the vapor release, a high-power, continuous-wave (CW) laser beam
emitting at \SI{1064}{\nano\meter} (delivering up to \SI{6}{\watt} focused to a
\SI{200}{\micro\meter} waist) is targeted directly onto the opaque surface of the
small pill for approximately \SI{20}{\second} \cite{lucivero2022}. This localized
optical absorption rapidly drives the internal pellet temperature into the
required activation window \cite{scherer2012, lucivero2022}. During this violent
thermal flash, the active Zr/Al matrix performs a dual function: it undergoes a
chemical reduction that releases elemental alkali vapor, while simultaneously
acting as a high-efficiency getter material that irreversibly sorbs other
chemically active gases created during the reaction, preventing them from
contaminating the internal chamber atmosphere \cite{scherer2012}.

Following the reaction, the liberated rubidium or cesium vapor rapidly
evaporates out of the porous pellet matrix into the cell cavities. As the small
pill cools down to the cell's nominal operating temperature (\SI{80}{\celsius}
to \SI{100}{\celsius}), the oversaturated vapor condenses into stable metallic
micro-droplets within the reservoir cavity \cite{maurice2017}. These droplets
act as a continuous thermodynamic reservoir, maintaining a constant saturated
vapor pressure governed strictly by the operating temperature of the atomic
sensor, ensuring long-term frequency stability during room temperature or heated
operation \cite{knapkiewicz2018, lucivero2022}.

Simultaneously, the interaction between the activated zirconium matrix and the
nitrogen (\ce{N2}) buffer gas dictates the final internal pressure environment.
The high-temperature activation causes the native passivation oxide layer on
the zirconium particles to dissolve into the bulk material, exposing a pristine
metallic surface that behaves as a chemical pump. This active surface
permanently chemisorbs excess internal \ce{N2} buffer gas via the formation of
stable zirconium nitride (\ce{ZrN}) solid solutions \cite{knapkiewicz2018}. In
these microcells, \ce{N2} is primarily introduced to restrict the mean free path
of the alkali atoms, minimizing wall collisions and suppressing Doppler
broadening \cite{maurice2017}. If the initial gas pressure exceeds the precise
operational target, calibrated micro-flashes of the activation laser can be
subsequently applied to re-expose fresh Zr sites \cite{knapkiewicz2018}. This
enables an incremental, highly controlled chemical pumping of the \ce{N2}
until the exact sub-atmospheric target pressure is achieved, optimizing
collision dynamics without the use of external vacuum lines \cite{knapkiewicz2018,
lucivero2022}.
