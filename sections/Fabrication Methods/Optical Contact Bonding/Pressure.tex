\subsection{Pressure Treatment}
Applied mechanical pressure during the bonding phase acts as a catalyst by 
promoting elastic deformation of surface asperities, thereby increasing the 
degree of intimate contact \cite{mai2015, trinh2022}, as illustrated in 
Figure~\ref{fig:pressure}.
\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{Images/pressure.png}
\caption{Effect of applied pressure on the bonding process. Forces 
    perpendicular to the interface suppress void formation by closing 
    residual gaps between surface asperities. From \cite{mai2015}.}
\label{fig:pressure}
\end{figure}
This mechanical assistance accelerates silanol polymerization kinetics, 
enabling the formation of covalent siloxane bonds at lower temperatures 
and raising bond energy from typical room-temperature levels 
($<$\SI{0.5}{\joule\per\meter\squared}) to values exceeding 
\SI{2}{\joule\per\meter\squared}---often surpassing the bulk glass fracture 
toughness \cite{kalk2014}.

Pressure magnitude must be optimized carefully. Research on Borofloat 33 
wafers demonstrates that a load of \SI{0.07}{\mega\pascal} maximizes 
flexural strength; pressures beyond this threshold introduce stress 
concentrations that cause premature failure \cite{trinh2022}. 
Non-uniform pressure distributions pose additional risks: stress-induced 
birefringence and permanent wavefront distortion can compromise device 
performance \cite{bain2022, wang2014}. High-precision applications therefore 
require well-planarized pressure plates with peak-to-valley flatness on the 
order of micrometers, fabricated from stiff materials such as technical 
ceramics \cite{birckigt2021}.

Birckigt et al. \cite{birckigt2021} describe a setup in which convex and 
concave pressure plates---with waviness of \SI{5}{\micro\meter} and 
\SI{3}{\micro\meter}, respectively---promote radially outward propagation 
of the contact front during the initial bonding phase 
(Figure~\ref{fig:chucks}). This geometry mitigates the risk of void 
entrapment and introduces radially symmetric residual stresses that are 
more predictable and manageable than those arising from asymmetric loading.

\centering

\caption{Pressure plate geometry described by Birckigt et al. 
    \cite{birckigt2021}. The curved profiles guide contact front propagation 
    outward from the center, reducing the probability of interface void

\label{fig:chucks}
\end{figure}
