
\subsection{Chemical Treatment}

Once surfaces meet the geometric requirements described above, chemical 
treatment prepares them for bonding by eliminating contaminants and 
activating the surface at the molecular level. These two objectives 
---\textit{chemical cleaning} and \textit{chemical activation}--- are 
distinct in their mechanism but complementary in their effect.

\subsubsection*{Chemical Cleaning}
Cleaning removes organic matter, metallic ions, and particles that would 
otherwise prevent intimate contact. The most common agents, applied 
sequentially in order of increasing aggressiveness, are:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Detergents and Solvents:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Micro-Soap:} Eliminates heavy organic matter and solid 
        particles by reducing the surface tension of water, allowing 
        contaminants to detach and be rinsed away \cite{tuli2012}.
    \item \textbf{Acetone:} Dissolves fats, waxes, and photoresists by 
        breaking down organic polymer chains \cite{du2022}.
    \item \textbf{Ethanol:} Applied as the final solvent step; its high 
        volatility ensures rapid evaporation and leaves the surface ready 
        for subsequent acid baths or plasma activation.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stronger Chemicals:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Piranha Solution} ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$:$\mathrm{H_2O_2}$): 
        An extremely corrosive and oxidizing mixture, and the standard choice 
        for removing stubborn organic residues \cite{du2022}.
    \item \textbf{RCA-1} ($\mathrm{NH_4OH}$:$\mathrm{H_2O_2}$:$\mathrm{H_2O}$): 
        Removes light organic films and dislodges insoluble particles through 
        electrostatic repulsion \cite{kern1970}.
    \item \textbf{RCA-2} ($\mathrm{HCl}$:$\mathrm{H_2O_2}$:$\mathrm{H_2O}$): 
        Eliminates heavy metals and alkaline contaminants \cite{kern1970}.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

Ultrasonic agitation during the initial cleaning stages substantially 
enhances contaminant removal. High-frequency sound waves generate microscopic 
vacuum bubbles in the liquid through cavitation; their implosion near the 
glass surface provides a mechanical scrubbing action that dislodges sub-micron 
particles from surface pores otherwise inaccessible to chemical soaking alone 
\cite{menon1989}.

\subsubsection*{Chemical Activation}
For fused silica and quartz surfaces, activation consists of increasing the 
density of hydroxyl groups on the surface, forming silanol groups 
($\mathrm{Si\text{-}OH}$). When two silanol-rich surfaces are brought into 
contact, they establish hydrogen bonds across the interface 
\cite{gosele2006, kissinger1990}. Although these bonds are initially 
reversible and of moderate strength, they serve as the structural precursor 
for permanent covalent Si-O-Si bonds, which develop during subsequent thermal 
treatment (see Section~\ref{sec:thermal}). This progression is illustrated 
in Figure~\ref{fig:bonding_process1}. In practice, activation is most 
commonly achieved by soaking the glass in RCA-1 solution or by plasma 
treatment, the latter being described in detail in the following section.
