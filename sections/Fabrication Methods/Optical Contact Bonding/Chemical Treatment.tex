\subsection{Chemical Treatment}
Chemical treatment pursues two distinct objectives. The first is 
\textit{chemical cleaning}: removing organic matter, metallic ions, and 
particles that would prevent intimate contact regardless of how well the 
surface geometry has been prepared. The second is \textit{chemical 
activation}: increasing the density of reactive silanol groups 
($\mathrm{Si\text{-}OH}$) at the surface, which serve as the molecular 
precursors to the covalent bonds formed during annealing. Although both 
objectives can be addressed by wet-chemical means, they rely on different 
mechanisms and are best understood separately.

\subsubsection*{Chemical Cleaning}
Cleaning removes organic matter, metallic ions, and particles that would 
otherwise prevent intimate contact. The most common agents, applied 
sequentially in order of increasing aggressiveness, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Detergents and Solvents:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Micro-Soap:} Eliminates heavy organic matter and solid 
        particles by reducing the surface tension of water, allowing 
        contaminants to detach and be rinsed away \cite{tuli2012}.
    \item \textbf{Acetone:} Dissolves fats, waxes, and photoresists by 
        breaking down organic polymer chains \cite{du2022}.
    \item \textbf{Ethanol:} Applied as the final solvent step; its high 
        volatility ensures rapid evaporation and leaves the surface ready 
        for subsequent acid baths or plasma activation.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stronger Chemicals:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Piranha Solution} ($\mathrm{H_2SO_4}$:$\mathrm{H_2O_2}$): 
        An extremely corrosive and oxidizing mixture, and the standard choice 
        for removing stubborn organic residues \cite{du2022}.
    \item \textbf{RCA-1} ($\mathrm{NH_4OH}$:$\mathrm{H_2O_2}$:$\mathrm{H_2O}$): 
        Removes light organic films and dislodges insoluble particles through 
        electrostatic repulsion \cite{kern1970}.
    \item \textbf{RCA-2} ($\mathrm{HCl}$:$\mathrm{H_2O_2}$:$\mathrm{H_2O}$): 
        Eliminates heavy metals and alkaline contaminants \cite{kern1970}.
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
Ultrasonic agitation during the initial cleaning stages substantially 
enhances contaminant removal. High-frequency sound waves generate microscopic 
vacuum bubbles in the liquid through cavitation; their implosion near the 
glass surface provides a mechanical scrubbing action that dislodges sub-micron 
particles from surface pores otherwise inaccessible to chemical soaking alone 
\cite{menon1989}.

\subsubsection*{Chemical Activation}
When two hydrophilic glass surfaces are brought into contact, opposing Si-OH groups 
form a hydrogen-bonded network across the interface, mediated by a thin 
adsorbed water layer \cite{possl1999}. This initial network 
provides sufficient room-temperature adhesion for the wafer pair to be 
handled without separation. Although these bonds are initially 
reversible and of moderate strength, they serve as the structural precursor 
for permanent covalent Si-O-Si bonds, which develop during subsequent thermal 
treatment, as displated in \ref{fig:bonding_process1}. Since the number of covalent 
bonds that ultimately form is limited by the silanol density present at 
the time of contact, a surface with few Si-OH groups will produce a weak 
bond regardless of the annealing conditions applied subsequently.

\end{figure}
\textit{Activation} consists of increasing the density of hydroxyl 
groups on the surface by forming silanol groups ($\mathrm{Si\text{-}OH}$). I
n practice, the most common wet-chemical route is soaking the substrates in an RCA-1 solution.
This process relies on a dual chemical mechanism: the strong oxidizing power 
of $\mathrm{H_2O_2}$ aggressively decomposes organic residues into 
water-soluble compounds, while the alkaline environment provided by $\mathrm{NH_4OH}$ 
drives a controlled, microscopic etching of the silica network. 
This subtle dissolution cleaves the structural siloxane ($\mathrm{Si\text{-}O\text{-}Si}$) 
bonds at the outermost atomic layers of the glass, 
leaving the dangling silicon bonds terminated as highly 
polar silanol groups, thereby drastically raising both the 
silanol surface density and the overall surface energy \cite{mai2015}.
