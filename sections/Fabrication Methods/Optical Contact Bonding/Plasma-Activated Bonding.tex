
\subsection{Plasma Treatment}
Plasma treatment constitutes one of the most impactful steps in the bonding 
protocol, combining the cleaning and activation functions described above 
into a single process capable of yielding high bonding energies even at 
low annealing temperatures. In a plasma cleaner, a mixture of 
electrons, ions, free radicals, and photons acts simultaneously on the glass 
surface through several mechanisms \cite{rammhandbook, masteika2014, suniphd}, 
illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:plasma_act}:
\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{Images/plasma_activation.png}
\caption{Effects of plasma treatment on silica surfaces. Plasma removes 
    contaminants and increases the density of available silanol groups.
    Adapted from \cite{yu2023}.}
\label{fig:plasma_act}
\end{figure}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Removal of surface contaminants:} Plasma decomposes and 
    volatilizes organic matter, producing additional dangling bonds and 
    increasing the fraction of bonded area \cite{cho2001, tuli2012}.  
\item \textbf{Increase in silanol group density:} Plasma-induced surface 
    modification raises the concentration of silanol groups 
    ($\mathrm{Si\text{-}OH}$), which are directly correlated with bonding 
    strength \cite{rammhandbook}.
\item \textbf{Enhancement of interfacial diffusivity:} Plasma treatment 
    increases the diffusivity of water and trapped gas at the interface 
    \cite{suniphd}. Water evacuation promotes the conversion of weak, 
    reversible hydrogen bonds into stronger Si-O-Si covalent bonds, while 
    gas removal prevents bubble formation during annealing.
\end{enumerate}
Common process gases include $\mathrm{O_2}$, $\mathrm{N_2}$, and 
$\mathrm{Ar}$ \cite{suniphd, tuli2012}. Plasma power, chamber pressure, 
gas flow rate, and exposure time must all be carefully controlled, since 
overexposure increases surface roughness and degrades bond quality. 
\\\\
\textbf{ESPECIFICAR MES SOBRE ELS PARAMETRES}
\\\\
A critical procedural constraint is that plasma treatment must always 
follow all chemical cleaning or activation steps: subsequent exposure to 
chemicals can etch the activated surface and negate the benefits of the 
treatment \cite{neumann2021}. Conversely, brief exposure to deionized water 
(DIW) or ambient air after plasma treatment is beneficial, since water 
molecules attach to the $\mathrm{Si\text{-}O^{-}}$ groups and convert them 
into silanol groups \cite{yu2023}. The recommended workflow is therefore: 
(1) chemical cleaning, (2) chemical activation (e.g., RCA-1), 
(3) plasma treatment, (4) DIW rinse, and (5) pre-bonding and annealing.

Plasma treatment is especially valuable for low-temperature optical contact 
bonding in LWVCs: it produces bond energies exceeding 
\SI{1}{\joule\per\meter\squared} at annealing temperatures as low as 
\SI{100}{\celsius} \cite{eichler2008, yu2023}, well below the thresholds 
that would damage pre-structured glass components.
