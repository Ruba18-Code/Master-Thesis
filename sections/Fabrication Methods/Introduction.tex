\subsection{Initial Considerations \& Constraints}
Bonding glass and crystal presents unique engineering challenges due to the material's non-porous nature, 
extreme smoothness, and high surface energy. In industrial applications, selecting the right adhesive or 
process is critical for ensuring optical clarity, structural integrity, and durability against environmental factors.
\\\\
Some of the most common glass bonding methods are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{UV-Curing Adhesives:} These are one-component resins that remain liquid until 
    exposed to specific wavelengths of ultraviolet light. When this adhesives are exposed to
    UV light, a rapid polymerization process begins. This method offers strong, precise, and
    invisible joints, since the refractive index of the adhesive can be tuned to match the substrate's
    refractive index.
    \item \textbf{Anodic Bonding:} The substrates are heated to $300^{\circ}\text{C}$--$500^{\circ}\text{C}$ 
    while a high voltage ($\approx 1\text{ kV}$) is applied. Ions in the glass migrate, 
    creating a space-charge region that generates a massive electrostatic attraction, resulting 
    in a permanent chemical bond.
\end{itemize}
While this methods can be very useful for many applications, they present some challenges
when it comes to MEMs and Vapor Cells manufacturing. Adhesives present in the interface 
can easily leak into the sealed cavity and potentially contaminate the gas. This can
greatly affect the performance of high-precission devices (e.g. atomic clocks). Moreover,
some adhesives can heat a lot when a high-power laser interacts with them. This can introduce
stress in the glass cell, causing birefringence, thermal expansion and cracks. Most adhesive-free
methods, such as anodic bonding, require high temperatures. This introduces the need for 
more sophisticated ovens and increases the risk of creating stress in the glass. Moreover,
some coatings used in optical applications can not be heated to such high temperatures. \\\\
Modern solutions that can adress this two problems at once are \textit{Femtosecond Laser Bonding}
and \textit{Optical Contact Bonding}.
