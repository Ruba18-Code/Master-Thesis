Having established the fundamental theoretical principles in the preceding 
chapters, this chapter focuses on the practical execution and targeted 
applications of these methodologies. The implementation of vapor-cell 
technology involves distinct practical steps, moving from the physical 
assembly of the sensor hardware to its evaluation in specific industrial and 
academic case studies. 

To address these aspects, the chapter is structured around three specific 
developments:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cell Assembly via Optical Contact Bonding:} The first 
    practical requirement consists of closing and sealing the vapor cell. 
    To achieve this outside a cleanroom environment, an experimental 
    assessment of chemical and physical surface activation protocols ---utilizing 
    oxygen (\ce{O2}) plasma alongside piranha and RCA-1 cleanings--- was 
    performed to optimize the adhesion of fused silica substrates.
    
    \item \textbf{Feasibility Assessment for Industrial Scenarios (COMSOL):} 
    To evaluate the performance of these sensors in a non-ideal, unshielded 
    real-world application, a 2D finite-element simulation was developed. This 
    model investigates an eddy-current induction layout designed to detect 
    subsurface structural corrosion at the foot of operational railway tracks.
    
    \item \textbf{Laboratory Validation via Zero-Field Resonance:} To demonstrate 
    a real academic application, a series of quantum-sensing experiments 
    was conducted in a controlled laboratory environment, focusing on the 
    Zero Field Resonance (ZFR) to characterize the spin coherence 
    lifetime ($\tau$) of an alkali vapor cell.
\end{enumerate}

By combining manual surface preparation with numerical modeling and laboratory 
spectroscopy, this chapter provides a realistic overview of the operational 
steps and potential applications of vapor-cell magnetometry.
