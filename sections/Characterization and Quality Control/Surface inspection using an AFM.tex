\section{Surface inspection using an Atomic Force Microscope}
\textit{Atomic Force Microscopy} (\gls{AFM}) is a sophisticated type of 
scanning probe microscopy, which consists of a physical probe
that scans the specimen in order to produce images of surfaces. 
Atomic Force Microscopes can map the topology of a surface
with sub-nanometric resolution, which makes them an ideal tool 
for surface inspection in the context of atomic sensors, and
more specifically in the context of Optical Contact Bonding,
due to the strict glass roughness requirements of this method.

    \label{fig:AFM_tip}
\end{figure}

The experimental characterization presented in this work was 
carried out using the facilities of the Physics Department at the 
\textit{Politecnico di Bari} (POLIBA) in Bari, Italy. 

A brand-new Nanoquartz glass disk, with a specified Root Mean Square 
(\gls{RMS}) roughness of \SI{500}{\pico\meter} and a Total Thickness Variation 
(\gls{TTV}) of \SI{2}{\micro\meter}, was inspected using a Park NX10 AFM. The 
measurements were performed with a Park Scout 350 tip in \textit{tapping} mode 
(resonance frequency \SI{320}{\kilo\hertz}, scan rate \SI{0.5}{\hertz}). The 
sample was examined in three different spots of its surface to ensure 
consistency. The obtained data was subsequently analyzed using the 
\textit{Gwyddion} software, through which the two-dimensional 
(Figure \ref{fig:AFM_image2D}) and three-dimensional 
(Figure \ref{fig:AFM_image3D}) images were generated. The resulting density 
distribution of roughness is displayed in Figure \ref{fig:roughness_dist}, 
and the detailed list of the surface parameters estimated from these 
measurements can be found in Table \ref{tab:afm}. The precise definition of 
these parameters is provided in \cite{ISO}.

The results confirm that the Root Mean Square roughness of the disk is 
around \SI{500}{\pico\meter}, as suggested by the manufacturer. An 
alternative estimation of the surface roughness, known as \textit{Mean 
roughness} ($S_a$), was found to be in the same order of magnitude. 
Additionally, it was determined that the maximum height ($S_z$) is in the 
range of a few nanometers. The mean height is approximately zero, suggesting 
that the surface's defects are uniformly distributed. The outstanding surface 
parameters observed in this disk comply with Optical Contact Bonding 
requirements and therefore it is suitable for the fabrication of 
Laser-Written Vapor Cells and high-precision atomic sensors.
