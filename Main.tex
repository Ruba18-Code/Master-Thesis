\begin{center}
    \large\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}

This Master's thesis presents a comprehensive study on the design, 
fabrication, and characterization of Laser-Written Vapor Cells (LWVCs), 
a novel class of miniaturized atomic sensors inspired by MEMS technology. 
These all-glass devices confine alkali metal vapors in micro-machined cavities 
and are promising platforms for next-generation integrated photonics applications.
The work begins by motivating the need for compact, high-performance atomic 
sensors and introduces the LWVC concept as a scalable, chip-compatible 
alternative to conventional vapor cell technologies. The design section covers 
the key engineering constraints, the architecture of individual cells, and a 
wafer-level fabrication approach that enables parallel production of multiple devices.
\\\\
The fabrication methodology is built around two core techniques. The first is 
Optical Contact Bonding, a glueless process that joins glass surfaces through 
intermolecular forces alone. This requires extremely smooth surfaces 
(RMS roughness below 0.5 nm) and involves carefully sequenced chemical, plasma, 
thermal, and pressure treatments to achieve robust, hermetic bonds. The second 
technique is Femtosecond Laser Micromachining, which uses ultrashort laser pulses 
to create buried 3D microstructures inside the glass without surface damage. 
Post-processing steps include helium hermeticity enhancement via alumina coatings 
and alkali vapor activation.
\\\\
A thorough characterization and quality control section covers visual inspection 
via Newton's rings, bonding energy measurements using the double cantilever beam 
method, tensile strength testing, and sub-nanometric surface analysis using Atomic 
Force Microscopy. A spectroscopy-based leak test protocol is also proposed, 
xploiting the pressure-dependent broadening of alkali D-line transitions.
Finally, the thesis outlines several application domains, including magnetoencephalography, 
atomic clocks, space instrumentation, and rail corrosion inspection, with preliminary 
magnetometry experiments demonstrating Hanle resonance detection in a cesium vapor cell.
